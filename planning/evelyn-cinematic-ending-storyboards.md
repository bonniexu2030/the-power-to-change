# 381 Days — Evelyn Brooks: miniature-movie ending storyboards
**Status:** First storyboard draft for creative review, November 23, 2026. **Not yet implemented.**
**Approved creative direction:** Four cinematic beats per ending; **moderate motion (Option B)**; sparse captions, wide painterly compositions, quiet object movement, slow camera pans and dissolves. No automatic audio.

## Updated audio-and-animation direction — confirmed November 23, 2026
- **Visual style:** Silent-film-like miniature movie, not speaking-character animation. Keep portraits and painted figures still; emphasize cinematic editing, pans, zooms, gentle light, paper or curtain movement, and 4 shot progression. **No lip sync, mouth movement, or full character animation.**
- **Character dialogue:** Display each character's lines as elegant on-screen subtitles/captions. If audio is added, an **off-screen, character-appropriate voice-over** can narrate those lines while the figures remain still. Voice-over is **not yet approved or recorded**.
- **Voice consultation required:** Ask the user **before** selecting any voice or narration system. Review Evelyn, Ruth, Lottie Mae, Marcus, David, Daniel, Sarah and other speaking roles as appropriate: approximate vocal age, tone, gender presentation, pacing, emotions, accent/dialect, voice differentiation, narration style, and willingness to use synthetic voice. Do not assume caricatured Southern accents or stereotypes.
- **Accessibility and control:** Subtitles always visible; player-controlled shot pacing; if voiced audio is eventually approved, it is optional/mutable rather than mandatory autoplay. Respect reduced motion.
- **Example: “Ruth: What happens next?” / “Evelyn: We keep going.”** These words appear as captions. If the user approves audio, each could be narrated with its own chosen character voice, **without moving the mouth**.

## Why these three stories differ
Evelyn's **stat-derived epilogue** is selected by the existing game engine, not exclusively by her final Chapter V decision:
- **Still Rebuilding** (\`rebuilding\`): she continues coping with financial instability.
- **Community Ties** (\`ties\`): friendships and neighborhood networks remain important.
- **Finding Her Footing** (\`footing\`): she and Ruth are finding a steadier rhythm.

Her **final Chapter V choice** separately determines the insertion during shot 3:
- \`ride\`: Evelyn and Ruth go to the bus stop and ride the newly integrated bus together; Ruth chooses a window seat.
- \`wait\`: Evelyn talks with Ruth, makes a plan for a later ride, and maintains her ordinary work route that morning. **Do not portray them boarding the bus on that first morning.**
- \`neighbors\`: Evelyn and Lottie Mae support uncertain neighbors going to the bus stops; Ruth accompanies them when she can.

The cinematic follows the **December 20–21, 1956** transition. The boycott ends on December 20; integrated service begins December 21. Everything about the family's private actions and these dialogue excerpts is fictional.

## Cross-ending presentation system
- Canvas: wide 16:9 cinematic shot with light letterboxing; mobile-safe crop and subtitle safe area.
- Four beats, each click-to-advance, with optional 4–8-second gentle motion. Allow Back, Next, Skip cinematic, Replay, and show complete written ending afterward.
- Motion: slow 2–4% camera drift/zoom, subtle curtains, sunlit particles, paper flutter, cross-dissolves. Avoid character mouth animation, dramatic camera shake, flashing, or autoplay music.
- Character reference: approved Evelyn, Ruth, and Lottie Mae portrait designs remain canonical. New illustrative scene poses must follow those appearances.
- The ending text, choice consequences, relationship notes, historical afterword, reflection, journal and all save data stay available in their existing readable form after the cutscene. No change to \`finalEndingId()\`.
- Keep memory shots grounded in the *actual* earlier selections: \`walk\` → worn shoes on a long sidewalk; \`ride\` → handwritten ride arrangements; \`bus\` → fare coins with a paycheck. These are memories of coping strategies, not moral verdicts.
- Ruth relationship: show warmer body language when trust is high, thoughtful physical space when lower; do not imply estrangement.
- Lottie Mae relationship: show familiarity when high, respectful but somewhat formal distance when lower; do not invent reconciliation.
- Each cutscene finishes with its title card and transitions into the existing documented-history and reflection content. Historical victory must never depend on the player's score.

## Ending 01 — STILL REBUILDING
**Mood:** Bittersweet, exhausted but resilient. **Palette:** Amber stove light, dusty cream, cool December blue at the window. **Visual hook:** Bills and folded boycott leaflet occupying the same table.

### Beat 1: The morning after
**Setting/time:** December 21, early morning; Evelyn's kitchen.
**Camera:** Wide shot from the doorway, a gentle push toward the kitchen table.
**Visual:** Evelyn in her coat; Ruth's school things by the door. Newspapers and household bills lie beside the folded leaflet. Morning light enters from the window. A bus moves in the distance, not dominating the frame.
**Motion/sound:** 3% slow push; curtain shifting; no mandatory audio.
**Exact caption:** “The boycott had ended. The bills had not.”

### Beat 2: The weight of ordinary life
**Camera:** Three quiet close-ups joined by dissolves: wages/bills → folded leaflet → Ruth's worn shoes.
**Visual:** A 2-second earlier-choice memory appears as a small painted transition, never showing an action the player didn't take.
**Motion:** Slow camera track along table; gentle crossfade to the ch1 memory prop.
**Exact caption:** “Freedom mattered. So did making it through tomorrow.”

### Beat 3: The player's own next step
**Camera:** Medium framing, then a pan toward the doorway or street; insert is selected by \`choices.ch5\`.
- **\`ride\`:** Evelyn and Ruth walk to the bus, board, and settle together; Ruth chooses the window.
- **\`wait\`:** Evelyn and Ruth examine a simple plan together. Evelyn goes toward her ordinary work route; their first shared ride remains for later.
- **\`neighbors\`:** Evelyn and Lottie Mae help neighbors find their bus stop; Ruth joins when able.
**Motion:** Single sideward pan with a moving light layer, without new dialogue choices.
**Exact caption:** “She chose what she could do today.”

### Beat 4: Final tableau
**Camera:** Wide, nearly still view from inside the kitchen toward its open doorway; outside, life continues.
**Visual:** Whether Evelyn has left with Ruth, gone to work, or assisted neighbors, the table remains in view: the leaflet, the bills, and a shaft of warm light. Keep this shot flexible so it does not contradict the final choice.
**Motion:** 2% pullback, light gradually warming.
**Exact final caption:** “The law had changed. Rebuilding would take time.”
**Title reveal:** **STILL REBUILDING**
**Transition:** Dissolve into the original epilogue text, documented history, and reflection.

## Ending 02 — COMMUNITY TIES
**Mood:** Collective warmth without suggesting every difficulty has disappeared. **Palette:** Earthy ochre, warm cream, muted street blues. **Visual hook:** Folded transportation schedules shifting from urgent necessity to a record of neighborly cooperation.

### Beat 1: The neighborhood wakes
**Setting/time:** December 21 morning, street near Evelyn's home.
**Camera:** Wide street-level establishing shot, slight left-to-right tracking movement.
**Visual:** People prepare for work near a bus stop; buses run again under new seating rules. The camera finds Evelyn on the street or stoop; Ruth and Lottie Mae are nearby.
**Motion:** Gentle bus movement in background, subtle figures and coat hems.
**Exact caption:** “For 381 days, neighbors had kept one another moving.”

### Beat 2: What remains
**Camera:** Insert of a folded carpool roster and two hands exchanging a note, dissolving into the original ch1 memory detail.
**Visual:** No heroic speech or invented historical document. Use legible fictional record designs rather than pretending to reproduce a real archival sheet.
**Motion:** Paper moves softly; rack-focus-style shift from roster to people.
**Exact caption:** “The rides were changing. The connections remained.”

### Beat 3: The player's version of the morning
**Camera:** Medium shot, opening outward to include the relevant interaction.
- **\`ride\`:** Evelyn and Ruth go to the bus for their own ride. Lottie Mae and other neighbors may be visible elsewhere; **do not invent Evelyn assisting them**.
- **\`wait\`:** Evelyn and Ruth discuss their later ride near home; neighbors continue their own morning routines.
- **\`neighbors\`:** Evelyn and Lottie Mae answer questions and accompany anxious neighbors, with Ruth present when possible.
**Motion:** Slow lateral pan and quiet staging; keep the crowd small and personal.
**Exact caption:** “Support could take more than one form.”

### Beat 4: Final tableau
**Camera:** Wide, inclusive composition from across the street, framing home, sidewalk and the bus route.
**Visual:** Evelyn's neighborhood is the main subject. Evelyn, Ruth and Lottie Mae appear according to the selected insertion; spacing reflects Lottie Mae relationship without forcing closeness.
**Motion:** Very slow crane-like drift, then held tableau.
**Exact final caption:** “The movement ended. What they built together did not.”
**Title reveal:** **COMMUNITY TIES**
**Transition:** Original readable epilogue and documentary afterword.

## Ending 03 — FINDING HER FOOTING
**Mood:** Small, intimate optimism without overstating what has changed. **Palette:** Soft peach/gold in the kitchen, pale blue outside. **Visual hook:** The family doorway as a threshold between private uncertainty and public change.

### Beat 1: A familiar beginning, a different day
**Setting/time:** December 21, morning in Evelyn's kitchen.
**Camera:** Wide composition echoing Evelyn's first chapter: same table, same window, changed light.
**Visual:** Old leaflet rests beside neatly gathered work things. Ruth pauses by the doorway. The world appears ordinary, but the bus route outside now has different rules.
**Motion:** Slow push; curtain moves.
**Exact caption:** “The morning looked familiar. Something important had changed.”

### Beat 2: The questions they carry
**Camera:** Two-shot of Evelyn and Ruth at table height; insert of a folded leaflet and one choice-accurate memory prop.
**Visual:** Let expressions do the storytelling. High Ruth relationship: close stance or gentle hand on shoulder. Lower relationship: thoughtful, affectionate physical space, not hostility.
**Motion:** Very soft focus shift from the leaflet toward the pair.
**Exact caption:** “They had questions the victory could not answer.”

### Beat 3: A small decision, taken seriously
**Camera:** Medium shot that follows Evelyn's actions rather than a generic bus montage.
- **\`ride\`:** Evelyn and Ruth ride together; the camera briefly notices Ruth at the bus window.
- **\`wait\`:** Evelyn and Ruth discuss a safer future ride, then Evelyn prepares to leave for work.
- **\`neighbors\`:** Evelyn joins Lottie Mae to help others; Ruth accompanies them when she can.
**Motion:** 3% side pan and slow dissolve. Do not give \`wait\` players an immediate first ride.
**Exact caption:** “A new beginning could still be careful.”

### Beat 4: Final tableau and line
**Camera:** Return to a wide or medium-wide view from the kitchen doorway, echoing the first chapter. Evelyn and Ruth can appear here as a poetic cut back to the home scene rather than contradictory continuous action.
**Visual:** The door is open to soft daylight. A quiet, forward-looking pause between mother and daughter.
**Motion:** Light brightens subtly; camera holds.
**Exact final exchange as subtitles:** **RUTH:** “What happens next?” **EVELYN:** “We keep going.”
**Title reveal:** **FINDING HER FOOTING**
**Transition:** Original complete epilogue, historical afterword and reflection.
