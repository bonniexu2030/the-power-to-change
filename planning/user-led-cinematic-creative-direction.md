# The Power to Change — User-led creative direction (cinematic endings)

**Status:** Creative brief, November 23, 2026. **Not implemented.** These are user-directed decisions, not assistant approvals of invented details.

## Decisions made by the project creator
1. **Visual appearance:** Cinematic/movie-still look while retaining the game's **already-approved character designs, vibe and aesthetic**. Make scenes feel more dramatic and animated without replacing the cast's likenesses or stylistic identity.
2. **Scene construction:** Favor **full environmental scenes with the characters naturally depicted in the setting**, not merely static cutout portraits over backgrounds. Open to exploring more illustrations and layered animation.
3. **Evelyn and Ruth (refined by creator):** Preferred cinematic center is **A — Ruth in the foreground watching the integrated bus arrive, with Evelyn slightly behind her**; incorporate **C — a tender mother–daughter gesture / hand on Ruth's shoulder / shared closeness** as a secondary beat. **Mostly A, some C.** The image should suggest possibilities Ruth may carry forward while Evelyn continues her own work, without inventing Ruth's unplayed future or contradicting any Chapter V choice (especially wait vs ride). Exact emotional intensity and gesture remain for creator to decide.
4. **Marcus:** Current storyboard concept is liked, but the character needs **one unmistakable cinematic instant of discovering/finding his purpose**. Explore how this moment takes different shape across his three earned endings without contradicting choices.
5. **Daniel:** Needs **observable change and concrete steps after the boycott** instead of merely looking at a bus or thinking. The final montage must reflect actual decisions. A low-contribution/observer ending can show the contrast between *opportunities for change* and his *continued inaction*, rather than manufacturing a heroic reform. Sarah **appears physically** as an important independent influence in all three endings.
6. **Motion:** Blend **slow camera pans** with environmental animation, especially **a passing bus**, shifting light, and other gentle elements. **No spinning/rotating camera moves**, especially fast spins.
7. **Authorship and collaboration:** Creator **chooses scenes and emotions** and is heavily involved with **voice-over direction**. Creator does **not** want to write the narrative/story text or micromanage camera positions. Assistant supports craft, historical/branching consistency, technical staging, accessibility and implementation.
8. **Poetic elements:** Creator likes poetic expression through **images, motifs, color, rhythm, echoes, composition, and visual symbolism**, rather than being asked to write lyrical prose.
9. **Animation/audio:** The approved base is a four-beat miniature-movie cutscene with moderate movement, still illustrated characters, **no lip-sync**. Any later spoken words would be off-screen voice-over; **before selecting or generating narration voices, ask targeted questions about voice casting and delivery**. Captions must remain visible.

- **Ruth:** Black child's voice, emotionally mature/understanding, authentic childlike joy, never shrill or cartoonishly childish.

- **Daniel:** White middle-class man's voice, relatively neutral accent without pronounced regional markers.
- **Sarah:** Younger white woman's voice, appropriate to the 1950s setting, naturally warm; avoid affected "period" caricature.
- **All other speaking roles:** to be directed by creator when necessary, not silently assigned.
- Need additional approval/choice of *narrator* voice (age, warmth, weight, pacing), speaking style, final samples, accent authenticity and mixing *before audio is created*. No assumption that vocal racial identities can be determined by or reduced to accent alone.

**Creative responsibility:** These are the creator's selected emotions, music, scene beats, and vocal directions.
