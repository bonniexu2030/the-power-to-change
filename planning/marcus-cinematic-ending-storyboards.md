# 381 Days — Marcus Ellis: miniature-movie ending storyboards

**Status:** First creative storyboard draft · November 23, 2026 · **Not implemented in the game yet.**

**Approved direction:** Four cinematic shots per ending, miniature-movie composition, moderate motion (Option B), silent-film-inspired still characters. No speaking mouth animation or lip sync. Captions are always readable. If voice-over is considered later, **first ask the user** about ages, vocal texture, accents, emotion, pacing and whether different characters speak; do not choose or generate voices without consultation.

## Director-approved staging revision — supersedes earlier conflicting shot directions

**Signature moment common to all three Marcus endings:** an illustrated, movie-like sequence moving **from Marcus's desk → to the bus outside the window → back to his desk**, with a change in the light and his demeanor so the desk is suddenly filled with possibility. His emotion is **energetic determination, assurance and renewed ambition**, more than instant intellectual certainty. A short musical theme broadens with an elegant crescendo here. Do not animate his face or mouth; use two or three painted still poses and camera/paper/light layers. The sequence can occupy **one multi-layered shot** within the four-beat ending structure.

**Outcome adaptations:**
- \`costly\`: his returned glance still falls on genuinely unfinished coursework and the real sacrifices that cannot disappear; purpose = learning to sustain commitments.
- \`organizer\`: his glance returns to the community notes, showing his capacity to coordinate people and responsibilities; David appears **if materially helpful** to the partnership and relationship save.
- \`teacher\`: he returns to an open notebook, where the experiences of real people have changed what he wants to teach; **do not depict a future classroom as though he already has that job**.

**Final-choice fidelity:** \`teach\` shows teaching notes and a letter home; \`organize\` shows future organizing plans; \`balance\` shows a realistic division of coursework and community responsibilities. If a late choice seems to contrast with the earned ending, show both truthfully. **David should only appear where narratively important**—a relationship may appear through authentic notes/visual clues rather than a mandatory cameo. Visual poetry comes from notebook/paper/light motifs and the desk–window–desk rhythm.

**Score:** modern film-score piano and strings with a small jazz color; a brief statement of the game's shared theme, building with the *second glance at the desk* to satisfyingly assured optimism without swelling over dialogue. Environmental bus sound softly heard between music phrases. Narrator plus Marcus **tenor**, medium-volume, kind/intelligent character voice are potential recorded voice parts; details/samples to be approved by creator before audio is made.

## Story and continuity rules

The opening takes place **Thursday night, December 20, 1956, in Marcus's college room**. The boycott has ended, but buses do **not** begin integrated service until **Friday, December 21**. David has just brought the news; Marcus still hopes to become a teacher. The story respects the fictional character continuity and does not suggest Marcus alone secured the historical victory.

The game already derives one of three endings from **stats after the Chapter V decision**:
- `costly` / **Costly Commitment** when stability <= 40;
- otherwise `organizer` / **Emerging Organizer** when contribution >= 65;
- otherwise `teacher` / **A Future Teacher**.
Do not change this logic.

The player's separate Chapter V choice determines the **third shot's personalized insert**:
- `teach`: Marcus begins a letter to his mother and a teaching-note question: “Who keeps a movement going when the speeches are over?”
- `organize`: Marcus and David discuss continuing work on schools, jobs and fair treatment, with no quick-victory promise.
- `balance`: Marcus and David deliberately divide duties and protect time for coursework.

**Do not confuse ending classification with final choice.** For example, a player might choose `teach` and still receive Costly Commitment due to low stability. Use a physical moment and the exact choice meaning without pretending the outcome is different.

### Shared filming language
- Wide 16:9 images with a muted, illustrated 1950s treatment; cinematic letterboxing, mobile-safe captions, no modern objects.
- Approved Marcus and David portraits are the canonical references. Existing Marcus room, campus, and meeting backgrounds can be reused or adapted as layered cinematic plates; any new background must be reviewed before approval.
- Motion: 2–4% camera push or pan, light/cast-shadow movement, paper edges, crossfades and depth layering. **No lip sync, walking character rigs or forced character animation.**
- Four click-to-advance beats, each with Next, Back, Skip, Replay, and complete written epilogue afterward. No autoplay sound. Respect reduced motion.
- Softly show relationship continuity: high David trust → easy shared work or close framing; low trust → respectful space or a lingering pause, not fabricated reconciliation. Mother's relationship can be reflected by a recently opened letter or a still-unopened envelope; do not imply hostility.
- An earlier-choice visual memory in shot 2 must reflect the saved `ch1` and/or `ch2` selections: public leaflets, quiet assembly, or academic books; carpool schedule, study boundaries, or supply lists. Avoid depicting him taking a public role if he actually chose to step back.
- Preserve the existing `chapterFiveData.marcus.endings` full texts, both relationship notes, the `reflection` question, journal/saves, historical archive and post-cutscene navigation.

## 04 · COSTLY COMMITMENT (`costly`)
**Theme:** Collective work matters, and exhaustion and missed education matter too.
**Palette:** Midnight blue, amber desk lamp, tired gold.
**Visual signature:** One pool of lamplight over a crowded desk.

### Shot 01 — The news has landed
- **Visual:** Very wide, late-night Marcus's room. News of the boycott ending is on a folded newspaper; David has just left or stands quietly near the doorway (if present, reflect relationship closeness). Textbooks, study notes and volunteer leaflets crowd the desk.
- **Camera/motion:** Slow 3% push from doorway to desk; dim lamplight flickers almost imperceptibly; no character motion required.
- **On-screen caption:** “The boycott was over. Marcus's work was not.”
- **Story point:** No integrated bus is shown as operating on December 20.

### Shot 02 — The cost of showing up
- **Visual:** Low-angle insert across a calendar, incomplete class assignments, ride lists and a worn pencil; briefly dissolve to **one authentic earlier-choice prop** (leaflet stack, schedule, or textbook according to saved choices).
- **Camera/motion:** Slow lateral pan across the desk, soft edge blur to sharpen the uncompleted assignment.
- **Caption:** “Every hour he gave had come from somewhere.”
- **Emotional point:** Treat the tradeoff seriously, not as a punishment for activism.

### Shot 03 — What Marcus chooses now (final-choice insert)
- `teach`: An envelope to his mother and a page headed “Lessons I want to teach”; he makes an actual commitment to work through his studies.
- `organize`: Papers for further organizing lie beside a difficult, unfinished assignment; David can remain nearby, the shared future work real but demanding.
- `balance`: Two clearly separate piles, schoolwork and volunteer plans, arranged with deliberate boundaries; a note sets limited volunteer hours.
- **Camera/motion:** 2% downward tilt to the two types of paper; gentle depth shift.
- **Caption:** “He still had to decide what he could carry.”

### Shot 04 — Starting again
- **Visual:** Wide shot from behind Marcus, seated at his desk. Outside the window Montgomery's night continues; the room isn't magically tidy. He opens the textbook beside the faded leaflet. If David relationship is strained, keep the doorway empty; if close, show David's folded note or coat as a modest reminder.
- **Camera/motion:** Slow pullback; fade the lamp-light pool to a chapter title.
- **Caption:** “Commitment also meant finding a way to keep going.”
- **Title card:** **COSTLY COMMITMENT**
- **After:** Reveal unchanged full ending text, historical afterword, relationship notes, reflection, replay and change-perspective buttons.

## 05 · EMERGING ORGANIZER (`organizer`)
**Theme:** Marcus discovers the less visible skills of organizing: trust, coordination and sustained work.
**Palette:** Earthy parchment, warm lamplight, early evening blue.
**Visual signature:** A bird's-eye view of several hands and papers, not a heroic speech podium.

### Shot 01 — A room full of people’s work
- **Visual:** Wide shot of Marcus's room or small meeting space, filled with ride lists, scraps of directions and leaflets. Marcus and David are present as still silhouettes or portrait-consistent painted figures; the table, not the two men alone, fills the frame.
- **Camera/motion:** Deliberate pan from door to table, light over paper.
- **Caption:** “It had taken more than speeches to keep a city moving.”

### Shot 02 — The unseen labor
- **Visual:** A precise montage of original saved activity: public leaflets **only for `ch1=public`**, careful assembly for `ch1=quiet`, schoolbooks left beside a leaflet for `ch1=study`; one relevant Chapter II logistical object appears in the second dissolve.
- **Camera/motion:** Two crossfades over static illustrated details; paper corner flutters.
- **Caption:** “Trust grew through work that rarely made the headlines.”
- **Continuity:** His remembered role stays truthful to earlier choices.

### Shot 03 — What Marcus chooses now (final-choice insert)
- `teach`: Marcus points to his class notes and writes an idea for future students; David's presence remains a friendship connection, not proof he accepted new organizing duties.
- `organize`: Marcus and David review a page labeled “Schools · Work · Fair Treatment”; no promise of immediate success.
- `balance`: A short, sustainable volunteer schedule is shared with David, alongside a reserved study block.
- **Camera/motion:** Gentle top-down drift or slow reveal from hands to faces; not animated speech.
- **Caption:** “Leadership could mean helping others take part.”

### Shot 04 — A future shared with others
- **Visual:** Wide shot past the window toward the city and campus. Marcus's desk remains foregrounded, with more than one set of notes and the traces of other people's efforts. If David trust is high, their figures appear near each other; if low, their positions are a little more distant, without implying a broken friendship.
- **Camera/motion:** Slow camera rise / widening from desk to streetlights; fade to title.
- **Caption:** “The movement had changed his understanding of what one person could do—with others.”
- **Title card:** **EMERGING ORGANIZER**
- **After:** Existing full written ending and original historical/reflection screens.

## 06 · A FUTURE TEACHER (`teacher`)
**Theme:** Marcus can teach the story as collective history, not a single hero's victory.
**Palette:** Golden ivory, faded green, wood-brown; hopeful morninglight only if the scene transitions to Dec. 21.
**Visual signature:** A pencil hovering over an almost blank new notebook page.

### Shot 01 — The familiar room
- **Visual:** A wide, warmly lit view of Marcus's desk on December 20 at night. A leaflet from the boycott rests alongside textbooks and a letter from his mother. David's news is present on a folded newspaper.
- **Camera/motion:** Slow pan across the desk toward Marcus in profile; still posture.
- **Caption:** “A year ago, he thought he was preparing to teach.”

### Shot 02 — What history looks like close up
- **Visual:** A subtle sequence of memory objects: the exact type of Chapter I help he chose and a Chapter II note tied to his saved decision. On the desk beside them lie outlines for school lessons and the mother's letter. If her relationship is strained, let the envelope remain closed, rather than implying a new supportive conversation.
- **Camera/motion:** Dissolve across paper textures; very soft parallax.
- **Caption:** “Now he knew how much history depended on ordinary people.”

### Shot 03 — What Marcus chooses now (final-choice insert)
- `teach`: Marcus starts a letter home and writes: “Who keeps a movement going when the speeches are over?” in the margin of a lesson notebook.
- `organize`: Marcus and David discuss practical civic work beyond the buses; a teaching notebook sits nearby, showing a still-plausible educational future without inventing the `teach` choice.
- `balance`: Marcus writes a realistic timetable with both coursework and limited community commitments; he keeps a notebook open.
- **Camera/motion:** Slow zoom toward the notebook; caption enters like an old intertitle.
- **Caption:** “His education had become part of the story, too.”

### Shot 04 — A new page
- **Visual:** Close-to-wide final framing from over Marcus's shoulder. A blank notebook page is turned, then held still with the original leaflet just outside the central focus. The pen rests above the first line; the new page suggests a future, **not a cut to a classroom he has not yet entered**.
- **Camera/motion:** Gentle 2% push across the page; fade white-cream to title.
- **Caption:** “The next lesson had not been written yet.”
- **Title card:** **A FUTURE TEACHER**
- **After:** Full existing ending paragraphs, mother/David relationship notes, sources, and the question about who is recognized as a leader.

3. Is it important that **Marcus and David appear together in all three endings**, or should his more private endings focus almost entirely on Marcus?
